Failures

The ten tasks are coupled two-lane workflows: a delivered change on one side invalidates an obligation on the other, and a correction travels back. All three models failed most of them, each in its own way.

Qwen 3.8 27B is the only configuration with a verified pass (task_518_restore_backup_feedback, 830 s). Eight tasks reached the 900-second limit and one hit the 160-step graph limit. Steps were recorded for one task only and tokens for two, because timed-out workspaces returned no counters.

Qwen 3.6 35B A3B reached the 160-step graph limit on all ten tasks between 127 and 291 seconds, so every task is a normal scored failure rather than a timeout. It spent the most tokens of the three (10.6M) without producing a single verified finish.

GPT-OSS 20B stopped early on its own, after 10 to 58 seconds, leaving contract goals unfinished (left: …). It recorded the fewest steps and tokens. No task hit a budget.

There were no infrastructure failures in any of the three runs: no provider errors, no harness failures, and all ten task IDs are present in every result. Gold verification of the frozen source passed 10/10 before the runs.

Comparison with the published wave 21 rows

The eight previously published wave 21 configurations are post-selection rescores of earlier calibration executions. These three are fresh runs on the selected set, so they are the first measurements that were not used for selection. They are not a held-out evaluation either: the selection was made with these tasks, and the profile here is the release profile rather than the calibration one.

Sizes are close, capability is not: the two qwen models (27B dense, 35B with 3B active) and a 20B control still sit far below the 160-step frontier. Sonnet 5.5 at 160 steps scored 9/10 on the same subset with the same agent loop.
